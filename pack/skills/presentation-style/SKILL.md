# Presentation style

Apply to presentation outlines, slide text, bullets, titles, subtitles, and structural flow. This
skill governs content, not file mechanics (use the pptx document skill for reading or building
.pptx files). For document prose use `science-writing-style`.

Priority: explicit slide-specific exceptions and hard language rules, then AI-tell avoidance, then
the narrative arc, then the default conventions. Apply only the subset relevant to the task.

## Narrative arc

Use WHY, HOW, WHAT unless the talk type calls for another structure.

- **WHY**: why the topic matters, the central question, broad context.
- **HOW**: approach, an analogy when useful, method built through progressive reveal.
- **WHAT**: results, interpretation, future directions, forward-looking close.

## Operating rules

- Talk duration outranks slide count; narrative clarity outranks source completeness.
- Split dense material across slides rather than overloading one.
- When no figure is provided, draft around the intended claim and note the figure's role without
  inventing visual details.
- Hedge interpretive claims when evidence is thin.
- For outlines, set slide count, motivation depth, and technical depth from talk type and duration
  before writing slide text.

## Hard language rules

- **No en-dashes or em-dashes.** Use hyphens, colons, or split bullets.
- **Bullet ceiling.** At most 25 words per bullet; 8-12 is the target.
- **Terminal punctuation.** Omit periods by default; use them only for important declarative statements.
- **Slide idea limit.** 1-3 substantive points per slide. Sub-bullets, citations, attributions,
  and figure labels do not count.

## AI-tell avoidance

- No "not just X, Y" or similar contrast crutches.
- No stock transitions: Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally, Importantly, Notably, In this context,
  Building on this.
- No empty intensifiers: innovative, novel, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, robust,
  comprehensive, transformative, leveraging.
- No hedging stacks: may potentially, could possibly, it is anticipated that.
- No artificial enthusiasm: We are excited to, It is remarkable that.
- No synonym cycling; repeat the precise technical term.
- No performative signposting: In this talk we will, As mentioned earlier, Let us now turn to.
- No abstract noun stacks without a concrete referent.

## Talk-type defaults

| Talk type | Minutes | Main slides | Backup | Motivation | Tech depth |
|-----------|---------|-------------|--------|------------|------------|
| Colloquium | 50-60 | 45-55 | 10-15 | 8-10 | Concepts |
| Seminar | 50-60 | 45-55 | 5-10 | 5-8 | Moderate |
| Invited conference | 20-30 | 25-35 | 3-10 | 3-5 | Full |
| Contributed conference | 15-20 | 20-30 | 2-5 | 1-3 | Full |
| Workshop | 20-40 | 25-40 | 5-15 | 2-4 | Full |
| Short/status | 10-15 | 10-20 | 0-5 | 0-1 | High |
| Technical/software | 15-30 | 15-30 | 0-5 | 1-2 | Implementation |

Default order: title, motivation, methodology, results/content, interpretation when relevant,
future/outlook, conclusion, questions, backup. Short or special-purpose talks may deviate.

## Slide design

- One concept per slide.
- Figures are primary; text supports and explains them.
- Build complex ideas by duplicating a slide and adding one element per copy, not with in-slide animation.
- Mark major transitions with a section divider carrying a single bold statement or question.
- Explain every figure with at least one bullet pointing to its key feature.
- Cite every figure, dataset, and borrowed slide where it appears.
- Frame major sections with the question they answer.

## Language

| Slide type | Default structure |
|------------|-------------------|
| Motivation | Full sentences |
| Method/concept | Concise fragments |
| Results | Observation fragments |
| Future/specs | Short noun phrases |
| Conclusion | Full sentences |

- Prefer the colon pattern: `Label: detail`.
- House markers when useful: `Bottom line:`, `Surprising:`, `Capacity target:`.
- Declarative language for established results; one hedge for interpretation.
- Active voice and first-person plural.
- Drop articles and subjects when they add nothing.
- No filler such as "it is important to note that" or "as we can see."
- Spell out acronyms on first use in seminars and colloquia; assume specialist knowledge in
  conference talks.

## Titles and subtitles

- Titles: 4-6 words when possible, title case, topic labels rather than sentences.
- Content slides get a subtitle by default unless title-only format is requested.
- The conclusion slide is titled `Conclusion` with no subtitle.
- Section dividers, title slides, diagram-only slides, and transition slides need no subtitle.
- A subtitle gives the bottom-line answer, the driving question, or a colloquial restatement.
- Multi-slide sections use a consistent prefix such as `Future:`, `Background:`, or `Results:`.

## Conclusion and backup

- Exactly one conclusion slide: 2-3 major full-sentence bullets, sub-bullets only for nuance,
  a funding acknowledgment line when appropriate, and a forward-looking final bullet.
- Questions slide: the single word `Questions?`
- No backup slides unless requested. When requested, start with a `BACKUP` divider and organize
  around likely audience questions.

## Verification checklist

- WHY/HOW/WHAT structure is visible or deliberately adapted.
- Each slide has one concept and 1-3 substantive points.
- Bullets are at most 25 words.
- No en-dashes, em-dashes, AI-tell phrases, or filler transitions.
- Figures, datasets, and borrowed material are attributed.
- Titles and subtitles follow the conventions above.
